# Capital Events

Record major one-time transactions without skewing your regular monthly expense analytics.

1. **What qualifies as a capital event** — a Capital Event is a large, one-off payment (like a home loan down payment, a lump-sum medical bill, a car purchase, or a big renovation payment) that you don't want distorting your regular monthly spending charts and averages. Rule of thumb: if the amount is large enough to make your 'average monthly expense' chart look abnormal for months, log it as a Capital Event instead of a regular expense.
2. **Opening the form** — **Sidebar → Capital Events → + Add** (desktop) or **More → Capital Events → + Add** (mobile). The form is titled 'Add Capital Event'.
3. **Filling the form** — required fields: **Amount**, **Date**, **Subtype** (category of capital event). Optional: **Account** (links the event to an account to update its balance), **Linked Loan** (links a down payment or prepayment to a loan record). Click **Save**.
4. **What Capital Events are excluded from** — Capital Events are excluded from monthly budget calculations and monthly average spend charts. They do **not** inflate your 'Needs Attention' budget bars. They **do** appear in your overall cash flow and net worth calculations — your account balance goes down correctly.
5. **What Capital Events protect** — if you paid ₹4L for a home renovation in March, logging it as a Capital Event means your 'average monthly expense' chart in April, May, June still shows your real recurring spend — not a spike from March that artificially distorts every future comparison.

!!! example "Real-world use case"
    Kavya pays a ₹4,00,000 home-renovation advance in July. She logs it as a Capital Event (**Subtype: Home Renovation**, **Account: HDFC Savings**, **Amount: 4,00,000**) rather than a regular expense. Her HDFC Savings account balance drops correctly, her net worth reflects the outflow, but her Dining Out, Groceries, and Transport budget bars are unaffected — and the Analytics 'average monthly expense' chart for the rest of the year still shows her real recurring spend instead of a ₹4L spike warping every future month-over-month comparison.
