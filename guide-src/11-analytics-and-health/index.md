# Analytics, Trends & Financial Health

The Analytics and Trends pages give you a detailed, data-driven summary of your financial health and patterns.

1. Reading the Financial Health score — navigate to **Sidebar → Analytics** (desktop) or **More → Analytics** (mobile). The **Health Score** card (0–100) is at the top. The score is calculated from your YTD savings rate:
   - Score **10** — savings rate is negative (spending more than income)
   - Score **40** — savings rate is 0–20% (positive but low)
   - Score **70 (Stable)** — savings rate is 20–30%
   - Score **95 (Wealth Builder)** — savings rate is ≥30%
   Expand the **Health Breakdown** section to see four component metrics: **Savings Rate** (% of income saved YTD), **Expense Growth** (current monthly average vs your 3-month historical average), **Consistency** (how many of the last 10 months had positive savings), and **Risk Buffer** (months of runway: YTD savings ÷ average monthly expense).
2. Understanding Total Income / Expenses / Invested and Savings Rate — the Analytics page shows YTD totals for Income, Expenses, and Invested amounts, plus a **Savings Rate** (YTD). These are factual summaries of your data — not predictions or advice.
3. Reading Trends — navigate to **Sidebar → Trends** (desktop) or **More → Analytics → Trends** (mobile, URL /trends/). The Trends page shows:
   - **Category Creep Detection** — identifies categories where your spend has grown significantly compared to a previous period
   - **Asset Allocation Health** — shows how your wealth is distributed across cash, fixed-income, and market-linked investments, with a flag if too much is sitting idle in low-yield accounts
4. Using year / date selectors — both Analytics and Trends have year and date range selectors at the top. Use these to compare your current year's performance against a prior year, or zoom into a specific period.
5. An important caveat on all insights — the Financial Health score, the insights panel, and all Trends patterns are **descriptive summaries of your own historical data**. They identify what has happened in your records. They are not financial advice and are not predictions with any guarantee. Use them as data-informed prompts for your own judgment.

!!! example "Real-world use case"
    Nisha is considering applying for a personal loan and wants to understand her financial standing before approaching a lender. She opens **Analytics** and checks her Financial Health score — it shows 40 (savings rate 12%, 'Needs Improvement'). She then opens **Trends → Asset Allocation Health** and sees an 'Over-idle' flag: ₹1.8L sitting in a Savings Account earning 3% p.a. She transfers ₹80,000 into her Mutual Fund account (via **Add → Internal Transfer**), which shifts her asset allocation score. Two months later, with her savings rate up to 22% and the idle-cash flag resolved, her Health Score moves to 70 — 'Stable' — a cleaner picture to present alongside her loan application.
